Reading, writing, and math

What the videos actually say

These are posts about Generation Alpha, the children in school now. Generation Beta, the usual name for babies born from 2025 on, is still too young to be in these classrooms, and none of the clips below are about them. Each note is what the speaker says, not a finding of this brief. Instagram did not turn up a public reel on this topic that could be opened and checked, so it is not listed.

YouTube

Gen alpha can’t read, but its okay because we have AI now

Ashley Embers · YouTube

A first-grade teacher in the clip says she had to go back to the letter H, because children were not yet writing simple words. A fifth-grade teacher says students are reading at a second- or third-grade level. An eight- or nine-year-old, handed a menu, tells a bartender, “I can’t read.” The video cites national figures: in 2022 nearly 70 percent of eighth graders scored below proficient in reading, and about 30 percent of those scored below basic. For math, it says 56 percent of fourth graders were on grade level in spring 2023, down from 69 percent in 2019. The closing claim is that AI lets students skip the practice those skills take.

Gen Alpha still can’t read, and Gen Z needs professors to read to them

Teacher Therapy · YouTube · An ex-teacher who taught grades 4–8

She plays a fourth-grade teacher who has two students at a kindergarten reading level and about four at a first-grade level, with about six of 24 on grade level. The teacher says she is not allowed to use anything except the district curriculum, and there is no time in the day to read a book. The host says almost 40 percent of fourth graders score below basic in reading, and about 45 percent of twelfth graders do too, the lowest since 1992. She argues the drop comes from curriculums that dropped phonics, from passing students along, and from homes where nobody reads aloud. Her ask is 15 to 30 minutes of reading at home.

Gen Alpha can’t read and teachers are fed up

Informed Insights · YouTube

A teacher says children cannot read and cannot do math, and that blaming parents or blaming teachers will not change it. She describes buying a book set herself because the assigned books were not ones the students would read. She teaches in Florida and says banning a book does not matter if the child cannot read it. The plea is to teach reading first, then argue about which books.

Gen Alpha still can’t read and it’s getting worse

Casey Simpson · YouTube

He treats the reading trouble as a symptom. In his telling, a student given a paragraph cannot say what it was about, and some cannot say what a paragraph is. He also says a single required curriculum, used with every child, is why teachers resent the job and leave.

Gen Alpha still can’t read, and teachers are quitting

Marissa Van · YouTube

The video’s own description says teachers are leaving because of phones, TikTok, and AI in class, and because test scores are falling. It frames the reading problem as part of students who cannot focus once the screen is gone.

TikTok

Let’s chat

QBSkiiii · @qbthedon · September 19, 2023 · Atlanta-area seventh grade

This is the clip other teachers stitched. He says the pandemic put students behind, and that almost nobody is saying how far. Most of his seventh graders, he says, are at a fourth-grade level, and some are at kindergarten through third grade. He says he can count on one hand how many are on grade level, and that zeros in the grade book do not stop the school from sending the child to eighth grade.

I don’t blame the kids. I blame the adults.

Finally An EdD · September 20, 2023 · A stitch of the video above

A middle-school special-education teacher answers him. She says the children are not the ones who chose the reading curriculum. On the video she writes that seventh graders tell her they have never read a whole book. The comments under it argue about phonics: several teachers say sight-word guessing replaced sounding words out, and that children then could not read a new word.

How come kids no longer know how to read?

ryuhhnn · November 1, 2023 · A stitch of Newman Music Academy

The answer in the clip is phonics. It points at the Sold a Story argument: children were taught to guess a word from the picture or the first letter, which is not the same as reading it. Comments from teachers say a child who had learned to sound words out went backward after a sight-word program, and that a stepdaughter’s memorized books hid the fact that she could not read.

If your child is struggling with reading, there’s usually a reason

The Literacy Bar · A literacy coach, not a “Gen Alpha” rant

This one pushes the other direction. The coach says a child who hates reading often has the wrong book, or never learned that sounds connect to letters. The offer is practical: tell her the child’s age and the stuck point. It is useful next to the rant videos because it treats the same symptom as a missing skill, not a broken generation.

The videos stay on YouTube and TikTok. This page only says what is in them.
