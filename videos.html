YouTube’s co-founder on short videos

Steve Chen · Stanford Graduate School of Business · YouTube

Chen, who co-founded YouTube, says short-form video is entertainment for a moment, and that shorter clips equate to shorter attention spans. He says he does not want his own children to have short videos as their only way of watching, and then be unable to stay with something longer than about 15 minutes. He says children who are not shown the short, bright clips first stay content with the longer kind.

What Norway’s iPad study found about reading and math

Ryann Sutera · Support the Spectrum · YouTube · October 19, 2026 · Bærum, Norway

She says the online story, that Norway gave children iPads in 2016 and their vocabulary and literacy declined, leaves out the mixed results. In Bærum, schools piloted one-to-one iPads in 2015 and expanded that in 2016. Researchers compared those schools with other schools in the same municipality. The first evaluation used 15,708 tests and surveys, and it was not a random experiment. In first and second grade they found no significant overall effect on reading, spelling, comprehension, or mathematics. Third grade showed a negative effect in mathematics. Fifth-grade tests showed some positive effects in mathematics and English, particularly among boys, and ninth grade showed no significant effect on reading or mathematics. She says the first look covered only the first nine months, and a later cohort again found no clear effect on early-grade reading, spelling, or comprehension that could be pinned on the iPads. Norway kept digital learning in the classroom. She also cites a 2020 review of 42 studies, covering more than 18,000 children, which found that more screen use lined up with somewhat lower language skills, while educational programs and watching with a caregiver lined up with stronger language skills. She says that does not mean a screen causes a language delay. A child who already has trouble with language may get more screen time.

A 50-minute math lesson in short intervals

Dr. Michael McDowell · Cool Cat Teacher · YouTube · October 23, 2026

He says students and teachers get bored and frustrated when a lesson is split into thirds, an I do, a we do, and a you do, because that wears the battery down. He says Gloria Mark’s work is about where a 45-second average would put it, and that what he saw in the research hovered between about two and a half and three minutes before attention drifted into boredom or frustration. In a 50-minute math class he starts with two or three minutes of an ungraded quiz, then a silent protocol: he works a decimals problem without talking, and students turn to a neighbor and say what he just did. A direct demonstration stays a couple of minutes. He puts a dot on another class’s work and asks whether the dot marks something done well or something to correct. The habit that widens the gap, he says, is rescuing a struggling student by explaining the same thing again, so the second half of class becomes tutoring for 31 students.
